1. DEVELOPMENT-ONLY

Work ONLY in the Replit Development environment.

DO NOT Publish.

Do not modify Production database, Production schema, Production data, Production secrets, or deployment settings.

Do not attempt to fix or bypass the existing Replit Publish/schema-diff generator issue.

Preserve all existing functionality, data, permissions, workflows, NFC architecture, automated academic calendars, finance system, Parent Communication Centre, attendance, academics and lesson-note workflows.

Do not lock a school merely because a new term has started.

8. AUTOMATIC CARD LOCK

When the subscription enforcement date has passed and the required subscription remains unpaid:

Automatically place all relevant student and teacher NFC cards belonging to the affected school into a subscription-restricted/locked state.

Do NOT:

- delete the card
- delete the person
- delete attendance history
- delete student records
- delete teacher records
- delete card history
- permanently revoke the card
- destroy NFC UID associations

The lock must be reversible.

Conceptually:

Active Card
     ↓
Subscription Enforcement
     ↓
Subscription Unpaid
     ↓
Subscription-Locked

When the subscription becomes valid:

Subscription Paid
     ↓
Verification
     ↓
Subscription-Locked Card
     ↓
Automatically Restored

---

9. DISTINGUISH SUBSCRIPTION LOCK FROM CARD REVOCATION

This is critical.

An NFC card can already have legitimate states such as:

- Active
- Lost
- Revoked
- Reassigned
- Expired
- Replacement
- Subscription Locked

Do not overwrite permanent card status.

A subscription lock should be an independent restriction.

For example:

Card Status: ACTIVE
Subscription Restriction: LOCKED

rather than changing the permanent card status to "REVOKED".

If the card was already revoked for another legitimate reason, paying the subscription must NOT automatically reactivate that card.

Only remove the subscription restriction.

---

10. NFC DEVICE LOCK

Apply the same subscription enforcement concept to devices/readers attached to the affected school.

Do not physically destroy or delete the device registration.

Use a reversible state such as:

Device operational state:
SUBSCRIPTION_LOCKED

The reader/device should reject authorization events from the affected school while subscription enforcement is active.

Preserve:

- device record
- device ID
- school association
- configuration
- audit history
- NFC card associations
- historical events

When subscription is restored, remove only the subscription restriction.

---

11. NFC TAP BEHAVIOUR

When a subscription-locked card is tapped:

The system must NOT process the tap as a normal authorized attendance/security event.

Return a clear result such as:

SUBSCRIPTION_REQUIRED

The device/application should show an appropriate message such as:

School subscription is required. Please contact the school administrator.

Do not expose confidential financial information to students.

Log the blocked attempt safely.

Do not create a normal attendance record for a blocked subscription event.

---

12. TEACHER ACCESS RESTRICTIONS

When a school's subscription is locked:

Teachers belonging to that school must be restricted from subscription-dependent functionality.

At minimum:

Block

- Marking attendance
- Using NFC attendance
- Writing/submitting lesson notes
- Accessing relevant subscription-protected school information
- Other school operational functions designated as subscription-dependent

Allow

The teacher should still be able to:

- Sign in
- See a clear subscription restriction message
- See basic account information
- See the reason for the restriction
- See the school/contact information necessary to resolve it

Do not unnecessarily destroy or hide historical records.

---

13. TEACHER LESSON NOTE LOCK

A subscription-locked school must prevent teachers from creating or submitting new lesson notes.

If a teacher attempts to access:

Teacher → My Curriculum → Lesson Notes

show a clear message:

School subscription is required to continue using this feature. Please contact your School Administrator.

Existing historical lesson notes must remain preserved.

Do not delete previously submitted or approved lesson notes.

---

14. STUDENT RESTRICTIONS

15. PARENT RESTRICTIONS

- Subscription-protected academic information

The parent should still be able to:

- See basic account information

Do not delete:

- Parent account
- Parent-child relationship
- Previous messages
- Notification history

- Previous results
- Financial records

29. TENANT ISOLATION

This is mandatory.

A subscription lock for:

School A

must never affect:

School B

Test cross-school access attempts.

A Parent must only be restricted according to the subscription status of the school associated with the relevant child.

If a parent has children in multiple schools, handle each school's subscription state independently.

Example:

Parent
 ├── Child A → School A → Paid → Active
 └── Child B → School B → Unpaid → Restricted

Do NOT lock the parent's entire account simply because one school is unpaid.

---

30. MULTIPLE-SCHOOL PARENT CASE

This is especially important.

A parent can have children attending different schools.

Therefore subscription restrictions must be scoped to:

School + Student

rather than blindly locking the entire parent account.

The parent should still access the active child's permitted information.

The unpaid school's child remains restricted.

---

31. TERM TRANSITION

When a new term starts:

Do not immediately lock anyone.

Use:

Term Start
    ↓
7-day grace period
    ↓
Subscription evaluation
    ↓
Lock unpaid schools

At the next term, the system must evaluate the new term's subscription independently.

Do not carry a previous term's payment incorrectly into the new term unless the existing subscription business rules explicitly define it as valid.

33. FAILURE SAFETY

If the enforcement job encounters an unexpected database/provider error:

Do not randomly lock schools.

Prefer:

Unable to verify subscription
        ↓
Do not change access
        ↓
Log error
        ↓
Retry safely

Subscription enforcement must fail closed only where the subscription state is reliably known to be unpaid.

An infrastructure failure must not automatically be interpreted as non-payment.
